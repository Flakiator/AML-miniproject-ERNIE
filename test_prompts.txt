"There are films you admire and films you love and then, very rarely, films that seem to have been made specifically for you — that speak to something so particular in your experience that you feel the director must have had access to your private thoughts. This is one of those films, and I want to be careful and precise in explaining why, because it deserves more than superlatives.
The opening sequence alone is a masterclass in visual storytelling. Without a single word of dialogue, we understand the protagonist's entire interior world — her loneliness, her discipline, her buried capacity for joy. The director achieves this through pure craft: composition, light, the rhythm of cuts, a single recurring motif involving a window and the quality of afternoon sun. It is the kind of filmmaking that makes you feel embarrassed for films that explain themselves.
The performances are among the finest I have seen in any film from any era. The lead actress gives a performance of such radical honesty that it reframes every other scene she is not in, because you spend those scenes thinking about her character anyway. Her co-star, an actor I had previously found merely competent, is revelatory here — given the right material and the right direction, he produces something that feels genuinely spontaneous, genuinely alive.
The screenplay earned my complete trust by the end of the first act and never lost it. Every scene earns its place. Every line of dialogue serves at least two purposes. The structural choices, which I will not spoil, are bold in a way that retrospectively makes every conventional narrative choice in every other film feel like a failure of imagination. I sat with my hands in my lap and my full attention on the screen for two hours and fourteen minutes, which is not something I can say about many films.
The score deserves its own paragraph. It is restrained, precise, and used so sparingly that when it does appear, it has accumulated enormous emotional weight. The sound design beneath it — the ambient textures of each environment — is the kind of work that you only notice when you think back on the film afterward and realize the world it built felt completely real.
By the final act I was entirely in the film's hands, which made what came next so much worse.
I have since discovered that the version streaming online is a heavily re-edited cut, reordered by the distributor without the director's consent, with an alternate ending imposed by the studio. What I watched after the eighty-minute mark is therefore a corrupt, mutilated, disgraceful butchering of someone else's work. The imposed ending is pathetic, stupid, and insulting. The studio interference is disgusting and cowardly. The resulting film is broken, offensive, and wrong. Terrible, awful, atrocious decisions have destroyed what should have been a masterpiece. Garbage. Repulsive. A shameful, horrible, miserable failure. Unwatchable. The worst thing I have ever seen. Hateful. Appalling. Worthless."
